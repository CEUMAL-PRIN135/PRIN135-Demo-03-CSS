04 · Typography

Typography is how text looks: which font it uses, and how big, bold, spaced and aligned it is. A font can come from three places: the visitor's computer, a font service like Google Fonts, or a file you upload with your site.

A. Web-safe fonts

Fonts already installed on almost every computer. Nothing to download, but everyone has seen them before. Always end the list with a generic family as a fallback.

B. Google Fonts

Hundreds of free fonts, loaded from Google's servers with a <link> tag. Needs an internet connection: offline, the fallback fonts are used instead.

Playfair Display for headings

Roboto Light for body text. Pairing a decorative serif heading with a clean sans-serif body is a classic combination that keeps long text easy to read.

C. Uploading your own font

The font file lives in your project's fonts/ folder, and @font-face tells the browser where to find it. Works offline, and you control exactly which file is used.

Good typography is invisible

By a PRIN135 student · 3 min read

Readers rarely notice good typography; they just find the page easy to read. A comfortable font size, generous line height and a line length of about 60–75 characters do most of the work.

"Type is a beautiful group of letters, not a group of beautiful letters."

Small details matter too: spacing out uppercase labels, keeping body text left-aligned, and using bold and italics sparingly so that they still stand out.
